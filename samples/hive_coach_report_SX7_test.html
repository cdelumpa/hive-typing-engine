SX Seven — The Enthusiast in Fascination — How the Instinct Shapes the Type
  • The SX instinct focuses the Seven's enthusiasm and hunger for possibility onto depth of connection and fascination — not breadth. This client is not looking for variety everywhere; they are looking for the person or idea that is genuinely, electrically alive. 'Deep conversations with people I find genuinely fascinating' is this subtype's natural habitat.
  • SX Sevens experience the world through intensity of engagement. When something or someone is alive for them, everything is possible. When fascination fades, the pull to move on can feel like a physical necessity. The 'scattered, not following through' feedback from others may reflect this: projects or relationships that have lost their fascination lose the client's full presence.
  • The SX instinct also amplifies the relational quality of the Seven's enthusiasm — 'getting the people I care about excited' is not just about sharing; it is about maintaining the charge between people. SX Sevens often need others to be as alive to the possibility as they are, which can create real connection but also restlessness when others can't match the energy.
  • Unlike SP Sevens (who hoard options and resources) or SO Sevens (who share enthusiasm outward as a form of service), SX Sevens pursue fascination as an end in itself — the depth of the encounter is the point.
Why This Subtype Can Be Easy to Miss

  • The typical misread is toward Type 4 — the focus on depth, the selectivity about who is 'genuinely fascinating,' and the intensity of engagement can all read as Four-ish.   • Another misread is toward Type 3 — the relational activation quality ('getting people excited about possibilities') can look like Type 3's desire to be impressive. The distinction is whether the client is energized by the excitement itself or by being the one who generates it. SX Seven tends toward the former.
  • The scattered quality named by others is easier to understand through the SX lens: it's not generalized distraction, it's that things which have lost their fascination are genuinely hard to sustain focus on. The follow-through failures are selectively connected to what no longer feels alive.
Coaching Notes

Try asking: 'Think about someone you've described as genuinely fascinating — what is it about them that made them that? And do you notice what happens to that quality over time?'
