1A · Counter-Type Considerations
Why This Matters
  • SP Four (Tenacity) is one of the most commonly misread subtypes in the system — it often presents as capable, disciplined, and self-sufficient, which can look like a Three, a One, or simply 'a healthy person.' If you open with standard Four descriptors (longing, melancholy, dramatic expressiveness), the client may not recognize themselves.
  • The SP Four's relationship to their depth is characterized by endurance rather than expression — they feel the full weight of the Four's inner life but process it privately and push through. This makes them harder to spot and sometimes harder for the client themselves to recognize as a Four.
  • The client's direct naming of envy is actually a counter-type tell: the SP Four notices the envy mechanism with a kind of wry, grounded clarity rather than being flooded by it. That quality of 'I see this happening in me' is very SP Four and worth naming explicitly in the debrief.
  • Framing the counter-type dimension early protects the client from feeling like the type doesn't fit — it opens the door to a more nuanced and ultimately more accurate self-recognition.
Standard vs. Counter-Type Presentation
  • Standard Four (especially SX and SO) tends to express emotional depth outward — through aesthetics, through relational intensity, through visible longing or melancholy. The SP Four holds that same depth internally and often appears more controlled, even stoic, from the outside.
  • Both share the core Four architecture: the sense of something missing, the orientation toward authenticity, the sensitivity to what is real and meaningful, and the structural wound of deficiency. What differs is how that material is metabolized and expressed.
  • Where SX and SO Fours may dramatize their inner life (consciously or not), the SP Four tends to discipline it — using the intensity as fuel for endurance, creative output, or quiet persistence rather than interpersonal expression.
  • The key distinguishing motivation: SP Fours still carry the Four's longing and deficiency wound — they simply refuse to be defeated by it. That refusal is the Tenacity, and it's what makes this subtype look like a Three or One on the surface while remaining fully Four underneath.
Coaching Notes
  • Lead with tenacity and inward resilience before introducing longing or deficiency — let the client recognize themselves in the strength first, then surface the shadow gently.
  • When you introduce the standard Four description, frame it explicitly: 'The SP version of this type often doesn't look the way Fours are commonly described — you may find some of the standard language resonates and some doesn't. That's expected.' This normalizes the discrepancy and keeps the client engaged rather than dismissing.
  • If the client says 'I don't feel dramatic or moody,' use that as confirmation rather than a challenge — the SP Four's relationship to their own emotional life is characterized precisely by that quality of not being overtaken by it.
