1A · Counter-Type Considerations
Why This Matters
  • SP 4 is the counter-type of the Four triad — the subtype that looks least like the standard Four description. Standard Fours are often described as emotionally expressive, romantically intense, and prone to dwelling in longing. SP Fours are more private, more driven, and more likely to fight the feeling of deficiency rather than inhabit it.
  • If you open with the standard Four description, this client may push back or feel unseen. The resonance door is more likely to open through the Tenacity frame: the persistence, the inward quality, the refusal to be undone, the drive to build something real in the face of what's missing.
  • The client's Stage 0 language already shows this: 'a persistent sense that others have something I lack, which can pull me away from what I actually have' — the word 'persistent' and the watching quality ('can pull me away') both suggest a Four who is in a kind of ongoing low-grade struggle with the deficiency feeling rather than swimming freely in it.
  • This matters for the debrief because it affects what evidence you lead with and how you frame the growth edge. For this client, the growth conversation may be less about 'stop dramatizing' and more about 'notice when the tenacity itself becomes a way of not feeling.'
Standard vs. Counter-Type Presentation
  • Standard Four presentation: emotionally expressive, prone to moodiness, draws identity from depth and uniqueness, longs openly, may dramatize suffering, identifies strongly with being different.
  • SP 4 (Tenacity) counter-type: internalizes the longing rather than externalizing it, develops resilience and stoicism as a response to deficiency, competes with themselves more than with others, can look driven and capable — which is why this subtype is sometimes misread as a Three or a One.
  • What they share: both are organized around the core belief that something essential is missing, both feel things very deeply, both prize authenticity and resist inauthenticity in themselves and others, and envy remains central — but SP 4's envy tends to be more private, more internalized, and less visibly enacted.
  • The key distinguishing motivation: an SP 4 is not primarily performing or achieving for recognition (Three) and is not primarily striving for correctness (One) — they are building something real and enduring as a way of proving to themselves that the deficiency is not the whole story. The doing is in service of the being.

SP 4 — The Creative Individualist — How the Instinct Shapes the Type
  • The SP instinct orients toward security, comfort, and the basics of physical and psychological survival. In a Four, this produces a subtype that channels deficiency inward and downward — building inner resources, enduring the pain privately, and developing a kind of stoic resilience that other Fours often don't show.
  • Where SX Fours tend to project their longing onto relationships and SO Fours tend to compare themselves socially and push to be recognized, SP Fours tend to compete with themselves — holding a standard for their own aliveness and authenticity that is private, exacting, and rarely fully met.
  • The SP Four's 'tenacity' shows up as a refusal to be defeated by the deficiency feeling — not by transcending it, but by working through it, building through it, making something real in spite of it. For an SP 4, this can be particularly subtle because the drive and self-sufficiency of the counter-type means the stress move may not be immediately recognizable as a departure.
  • The client confirmed this pattern cleanly in Stage 4. Watch for it presenting as a sudden shift from inward-focus to outward-focus — becoming helpful, accommodating, or relational in ways that feel slightly effortful or effortful-but-virtuous from the inside.

Wings — Type 3 and Type 5

  • The Five wing adds intellectual depth, a desire for privacy and self-sufficiency, and a somewhat more detached quality to the emotional life. For an SP 4, a Five wing can deepen the inward, self-reliant quality and add a more analytical relationship to experience.
  • Let the client lead — ask which description feels more like a resource, not which description fits. Both wings are available to every Four; the question is which one they tend to draw on under which conditions.
Try asking: 'When you're at your best — when you feel most like yourself and most capable — do you find yourself more drawn toward doing and achieving, or toward thinking and understanding? Which feels more like home?'
