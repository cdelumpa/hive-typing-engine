Standard vs. Counter-Type Presentation
  • Standard Four (SX or SO): emotionally expressive, romantically longing, visibly drawn to what's missing, relationship or identity as the primary arena of suffering. May present as moody, aesthetically preoccupied, or seeking to be witnessed in their depth.
  • SP 4 (Tenacity): the same deficiency story is present, but it's lived inwardly and pushed through rather than displayed. They work hard, endure, and build something out of their suffering.   • The distinguishing motivation: SP 4 is not pushing through because they're strong — they're pushing through because the alternative (being seen as needy, failing, collapsing into their pain) is intolerable. The pushing through is itself a response to shame about needing.

  • Fours manage this wound by turning toward depth, authenticity, and meaning — if I can't have what others have, I can at least have what is real and beautiful and mine. Identity becomes a project of self-creation and self-distinction. The idealized self is uniquely authentic, deeply feeling, and genuinely committed to what matters.
  • The core emotional move is longing — reaching toward what would complete the self — combined with envy as its shadow. Envy is not simple jealousy; it's a comparison mechanism that scans for evidence of deficiency and finds it reliably.
  • SP 4 adds a layer: the deficiency story is held with endurance and even used as fuel. Where SX 4 might dramatize the longing and SO 4 might suffer stoically in service of others, SP 4 pushes through — and the pushing through itself can mask the depth of the underlying wound even from the client themselves.

SP Four — Tenacity — How the Instinct Shapes the Type
  • The Self-Preservation instinct turns the Four's deficiency story inward and makes it a private endurance project.   • SP 4's attention goes first to security, sufficiency, and what they're building or protecting for themselves. This means the envy they experience is often less about love or recognition and more about capability or material groundedness — 'they have the life I can't seem to build' rather than 'they have the love I deserve.'
  • The self-sufficiency of the SP instinct can make this client appear more grounded than they feel. They may not invite support, not because they don't need it, but because needing support visibly conflicts with the SP imperative toward self-sufficiency.
  • This combination produces someone who works hard, endures difficulty with composure, and can appear to be thriving while carrying a significant internal deficiency story. The gap between the external presentation and the internal experience is one of the defining features of this subtype.
