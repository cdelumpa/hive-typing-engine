The Core Pattern

This is not primarily about hedonism; it's a head-center pattern organized around maintaining a particular inner state and moving away from what threatens it.
  • For the SO 7, the avoidance of deprivation is managed through meaning and contribution rather than personal pleasure. The Seven's hunger gets redirected: instead of 'I need to experience everything life has to offer,' it becomes 'I need to ensure that others have access to joy and possibility.' The self-depletion that results (burnout) is the avoidance strategy's shadow.
  • The Frustration triad (Object Relations) is important for this client: Sevens, like Ones and Fours, experience life as not quite delivering what they need — and the response is to keep moving, reframing, generating. For the SO 7, this frustration gets externalized as a drive to fix what's lacking in the world or the group rather than sitting with what's lacking internally.
  • Growth for this type involves learning to tolerate deprivation — to be with 'not enough' without immediately redirecting toward the next generative move. The security point (Five) is the invitation: what happens when this client actually stops?

  • Watch for the client intellectualizing the burnout — explaining it as circumstantial, attributing it to external demands, or immediately reframing it as meaningful sacrifice. That move is the pattern itself. Name it gently if it shows up.
Try asking: 'When you imagine actually stopping — not taking a break to recover so you can give more, but genuinely stopping — what comes up for you? What do you imagine you'd be left with?'
