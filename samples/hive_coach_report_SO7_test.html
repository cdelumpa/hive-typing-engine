1A · Counter-Type Considerations
Why This Matters
  • The SO Seven (Sacrifice) is one of the most commonly mistyped counter-types in the system — it is routinely read as a Two, and sometimes as a Three or a One. If you open this debrief with standard Seven framing (gluttony, scattered attention, personal desire, avoidance of pain), this client will very likely disengage or push back — and that pushback will be legitimate, not resistance.
  • The counter-type is not a partial Seven or a Seven who has done a lot of work. It is a full expression of the Seven's structure, shaped by the Social instinct in a way that redirects the Seven's expansiveness outward. The core fear (being trapped, cut off from fullness and meaning) and the core pattern (anticipating, reframing, keeping options open) are still present — they just show up in service of the group rather than the self.
  • For this client specifically, 'burnout' is the shadow signal. Counter-type Sevens can wear their self-sacrifice as identity, which makes it harder to see the avoidance of limitation and pain that sits underneath it. The burnout names the cost without necessarily naming the driver.
  • Getting the counter-type framing right in this session will determine whether the client recognizes themselves in the type — or whether they walk away thinking 'that doesn't sound like me at all' and quietly dismiss the whole exercise.
Standard vs. Counter-Type Presentation
  • Standard Seven: visibly enthusiastic, personally pleasure-seeking, oriented toward their own stimulation and variety, sometimes restless or scattered, future-casting as a personal strategy for keeping options open. The appetite is about what's good for me.
  • SO Seven (Sacrifice): the same underlying drive toward fullness and meaning, but channeled almost entirely into the group or the mission. Their joy is shared; their energy goes toward inspiring others; they describe themselves as devoted and selfless. The appetite is for collective meaning — but it is still appetite, still driven by a fear of limitation and loss.
  • What they share: the Frustration theme (the world doesn't deliver what it should), the Positive Outlook harmonic (difficulty sitting with pain or darkness, reframing toward what's good and possible), and the stress/security architecture (Type 1 under stress, Type 5 in security). These are the motivational anchors to test.
  • The distinguishing motivation from Two: the SO Seven gives because giving is where their energy is alive and meaningful — not because they fear they won't be loved if they stop. When the burnout hits, the Seven tends to feel trapped and depleted; the Two tends to feel unappreciated and resentful. Ask which of those rings truer.
Coaching Notes

  • When you introduce the counter-type concept, consider saying something like: 'There's a version of this type that most people don't immediately recognize — because it looks, on the surface, a lot more like a Two or a helper. But the driver underneath is different. I'd like to walk you through what I mean.' This prepares them for the potential surprise without destabilizing the recognition.
  • If they push back with 'that doesn't sound like me' when you describe standard Seven qualities, that's expected — say so. 'Right — and that's actually what I'd expect you to say. The version of this type I'm pointing toward doesn't match the standard description. Let me try a different door.'
