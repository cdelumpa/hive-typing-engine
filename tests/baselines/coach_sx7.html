The Bottom Line

This is a clean, high-confidence Type 7 — Sexual subtype, with unusually clear self-awareness of the core avoidance pattern. The most important thing going in is that this client already names their shadow precisely; the debrief work is less about revelation and more about deepening and translating that awareness into embodied practice.

What Test’s Responses Revealed
'Seeing possibilities everywhere and getting the people I care about excited about them' — this is the 7's core gift stated plainly, and it doubles as the idealization: I am the one who sees what's possible.
'Good at starting things and inspiring others but not always at following through' — the others' description names the structural consequence of the avoidance pattern: staying with the exciting start, leaving before the constraining middle.
'I avoid discomfort and difficult emotions I reframe or escape rather than sitting with what's hard' — this is the shadow named with unusual precision. The client has cognitive access to the pattern; the developmental question is whether they have felt access to what the reframe is protecting.
The Stage 1 open response 'when the spark fades and I'm already scanning for the next thing that lights me up' — articulates the Frustration triad's forward-motion dynamic with perfect clarity.

Leading vs. Alternate Hypothesis
The one pattern worth holding lightly alongside the Type 7 reading is a thread that resembles Type 5 — The Observer.
Type 7 - The EnthusiastLeading Hypothesis Type 5 - The ObserverAlternate Hypothesis
CORE MOTIVATION To be happy, free, and satisfied — pursuing possibility and pleasure while avoiding pain and limitation. To be capable, self-sufficient, and unintruded upon — protecting limited inner resources.
FOCUS OF ATTENTION Options, possibilities, what's interesting next, the pleasurable future, escape routes from discomfort. Understanding, knowledge, the demands others place on energy and privacy.
ENERGY GOES TO… Pursuing experiences, generating ideas, reframing the negative, keeping options open and moving. Observing, analyzing, conserving resources, withdrawing to replenish, mastering chosen domains.
GIFTS Optimistic, energetic, quick-thinking, adaptable, joyful, a synthesizer of ideas. Perceptive, knowledgeable, self-sufficient, objective, calm, deeply focused.
CHALLENGES Pain-avoidant, scattered, commitment-shy, prone to breadth over depth and unfinished pursuits. Detached, withholding of energy and self, prone to living in the mind over lived experience.
KEY DISCRIMINATORDoes the primary drive feel like conserving energy and depth of understanding (Type 5), or like staying ahead of limitation by chasing the next stimulating possibility (Type 7)?
In Test’s Words

“seeing possibilities everywhere and getting the people I care about excited about them”

“I avoid discomfort and difficult emotions — I reframe or escape rather than sitting with what's hard”

The client's language is consistently outward, connective, and generative — there is no signal of the Type 5's characteristic withdrawal, energy conservation, or preference for solitary depth.

Test Client · Type 7 – The Enthusiast
Debriefing Tips
Debriefing the One-to-One Subtype
Powerful question – “When the charge fades on something or someone you were totally in on — what do you do with that feeling before you move on?”
SX 7 chases intensity, not just pleasure — the connection or idea must have charge or it's not worth pursuing.
The all-in/fade-out cycle is the SX 7 signature: total absorption followed by restless scanning.
Relationships and projects are experienced as either electric or dead; there's little comfortable middle ground.
The idealization of the other (person, idea, project) is intense — and so is the disappointment when reality arrives.
SX 7 can look like SX 4 — the intensity and depth of connection hunger resembles the 4's longing.
The depth of engagement when sparked can look like Type 5 — intellectually absorbed, idea-driven.
Debriefing the Stress & Release Points
Powerful question – “When things feel stuck or constrained, do you notice yourself becoming more critical — of yourself, of others, of the situation?”
Under stress, 7s move to Type 1 — critical, perfectionistic, fault-finding.
For this client, the normally expansive frame contracts: possibilities narrow, flaws become prominent.
Coaching angle: help them recognize the 1-move as a stress signal, not the truth of the situation.
In security, 7s access Type 5 — focused, present, able to go deep without scanning.
For this SX 7, security might look like settling fully into a connection without needing it to stay electric.
Coaching angle: help them identify what conditions create enough safety to stop moving.
Debriefing the Wings
Powerful question – “Do you recognize yourself more in the loyal, warm, slightly anxious side, or in the bold, direct, 'I want impact' side — or does it depend on the situation?”
Wing 6 (The Questioner) brings loyalty, warmth, and a capacity for commitment — and some anxiety. When active, this client may feel more responsible and relational.
Wing 8 (The Protector) brings assertiveness, directness, and an appetite for impact. When active, the client may push harder, take more space, and resist constraint more visibly.
This client's SX instinct and the 'all the way in' language suggest the 8 wing may be more active — worth exploring.
Let the client lead: describe both wings briefly and ask which feels more like home, or whether they move between them depending on context.
