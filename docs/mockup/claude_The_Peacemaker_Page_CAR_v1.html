Development Ideas for Peacemakers

The Enneagram is a map and compass for developing yourself personally and professionally. Here are some ideas for bringing more courage, agility, and resilience into your life and work that can be applied in any situation.
Courage
Growing your capacity to experience fear, face what you avoid, and move forward anyway.
Peacemakers tend to avoid conflict, disruption, and disconnection. Developing courage means staying with that discomfort and trusting that right action will bring more connection, not less.
In conversations or meetings, try going first. Trust that your perspective matters just as much as everyone else's and is a gift, not a burden.
Pay attention to your “urge to merge” when you sense potential conflict. Trust that your relationship will survive moments of disagreement.
Seek alignment, not simply agreement. Trust that what you hold as important sometimes requires taking a stand. This is where win-win is born.
Agility
Growing your range of choices, especially when situations or circumstances get challenging.
Your Wings (Types 8 and 1), your Stress Point (Type 6), and your Security Point (Type 3) are resources that expand your range of choices in any situation or circumstance.
Lean into Type 8 when you need to hold your ground or assert your perspective.
Lean into Type 1 when you need to take a principled stand or stay in integrity with your values.
Lean into the higher side of Type 6 when you are overly anxious and need to reach out for help.
Lean into Type 3 when you need to find focus and move into right action.
Resilience
Growing your ability to get back up after a fall, dust yourself off, and keep going.
Getting back up after a fall means feeling all the feels and allowing the experience to teach you something about yourself you can apply next time.
Self-compassion can be hard for Type 9s. Try talking to yourself like you would to a friend.
What story are you telling yourself? Ask: Is it true? Is it really true? What if it is not true?
Remember: you are not alone and you do not have to go through tough times by yourself. Help is a phone call or text away.
The ideas above are a starting point for a deeper exploration on your own or with a coach. Take some time to reflect on where you want to focus and use the next page to capture your thoughts.
