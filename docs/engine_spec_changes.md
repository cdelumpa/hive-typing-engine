## Change 1 — Rewrite Task 3 opening guidance

### Problem

The current Task 3 instructs the Engine to begin the `client_narrative` with a formulaic opening: *"Based on your responses, the pattern that appears most consistent with your experience is Type X."* The HTML template also renders a static "A Note on This Result" block that opens with nearly identical language. Result: the first two sentences of the report say the same thing.

### Fix

The static "A Note on This Result" block will carry the framing language (see `report_template_changes.md`). The `client_narrative` should jump straight into what's specific about this client.

### Spec edit

**Current text (Part 3, Task 3, "Requirements" box):**

> Warm, specific, uses client's own Stage 0 words, non-pathologizing, frames type as invitation not conclusion. Always begin with language that holds the result lightly. Example opening: 'Based on your responses, the pattern that appears most consistent with your experience is Type X. This is a hypothesis — one that we hope will feel like recognition rather than assignment.' **If Stage 4 outcome is AMBIGUOUS:** do not name a type. Instead: 'Your responses reflect a genuinely complex pattern — one that resonates with more than one Enneagram type in meaningful ways. Rather than offering a premature hypothesis, we'd like to invite you into a conversation with Cai where this complexity can be explored properly.'

**Proposed text:**

> Warm, specific, uses client's own Stage 0 words, non-pathologizing. Do NOT open with "Based on your responses, the pattern that appears most consistent..." — that framing is handled by the static "A Note on This Result" block in the report template. Open with something specific about this client — their Stage 0 language, the texture of what they described, the particular quality of the pattern. Example opening: "The qualities you named — 'calming, grounded, easygoing' — are genuinely among the gifts Nines bring to the people around them, and the word you chose as your most problematic quality, 'quiet,' carries real depth here." The narrative should feel like recognition of this specific person, not like a generic type description with their name inserted. **If Stage 4 outcome is AMBIGUOUS:** do not name a type. Instead: "Your responses reflect a genuinely complex pattern — one that resonates with more than one Enneagram type in meaningful ways. Rather than offering a premature hypothesis, we'd like to invite you into a conversation with Cai where this complexity can be explored properly."

### Rationale

The opening framing ("this is a hypothesis, hold it lightly") now lives in the static block once. The AI-generated narrative earns its place by being specifically about this client rather than restating the framing.

## Change 2 — Add three new Task 3 sub-instructions

Three new fields need to be generated by the Engine. Each needs an instruction block added to Task 3. Add these as labeled subsections AFTER the existing `client_narrative` guidance and BEFORE Task 4.

### 2a. Core Motivation Evidence Paragraph

**Add to Task 3 as a new labeled subsection:**

> **Core Motivation Evidence — `core_motivation_evidence` field**
>
> Write one paragraph (3-5 sentences) that shows how the client's own responses align with the core motivation of the confirmed type. The static type description will appear in the report above this paragraph; your job is to point to the specific evidence in their answers that supports the hypothesis.
>
> Requirements:
> - Quote or reference specific Stage 0 language, specific Stage 1 answer patterns, or specific Stage 2 framework answers.
> - Use cautious language: "consistent with," "points toward," "aligns with."
> - Do not restate the static core motivation description — that appears in the report already. Your job is to show the evidence, not to teach the type.
> - If the content runs long enough to merit two paragraphs, use a blank line (`\n\n`) to separate them. The template will render each segment as a separate `<p>` tag. Don't let paragraphs run more than 4-5 sentences.
> - If the Stage 4 outcome is AMBIGUOUS or REDIRECT, this field should be `null`.
>
> Example for a confirmed Type 9: "The language you used to describe yourself — 'calming, grounded, easygoing' — points toward the Nine's core pattern of maintaining peace through accommodation and steady presence. Your most problematic quality, 'quiet,' is consistent with the Nine's tendency to route their own voice around potential conflict. Stage 2 also showed a Withdrawn social stance and a Positive conflict response, both of which align with the Nine's motivation to preserve inner and outer harmony."

### 2b. Personal Instinct Overlay

**Add to Task 3 as a new labeled subsection:**

> **Personal Instinct Overlay — `instinct_personal_overlay` field**
>
> Write one paragraph (2-4 sentences) that describes how the client's dominant instinct appears to show up specifically for them, based on their responses. The static instinct description will appear in the report above this paragraph; your job is to add the client-specific overlay.
>
> Requirements:
> - Reference specific Stage 0 language or Stage 1 instinct answer patterns.
> - Use cautious language.
> - Do not re-describe the instinct in general terms — that appears in the report already. Your job is to point to the specific texture of how this client's dominant instinct combines with their type.
> - If the instinct confidence is LOW (gap 0-1), note the ambiguity rather than overclaiming.
> - If the Stage 4 outcome is AMBIGUOUS or REDIRECT, this field should be `null`.
>
> Example for an SP 9: "Your description of finding calm in 'familiar routines and favorite weekends at home' is consistent with the Self-Preservation Nine's pattern of maintaining peace through the comfort of a well-ordered personal life. The self-containment you described — 'I don't need a lot from other people' — is characteristic of SP Nines, for whom self-sufficiency can quietly become a way of not imposing on the peace you've built."

### 2c. Secondary Type Hypothesis Narrative

**Add to Task 3 as a new labeled subsection:**

> **Secondary Type Hypothesis Narrative — `secondary_type_narrative` field**
>
> Write one paragraph (3-5 sentences) that describes the secondary type candidate ONLY IF the holistic analysis surfaced a meaningful alternative (`alternative_type_signal` in holistic_analysis is non-null).
>
> If `alternative_type_signal` is null, this field MUST be null. Do not invent a second candidate. Not every client has a meaningful second type — forcing one produces filler.
>
> Requirements:
> - Name the secondary type with its Hive name (e.g., "Type 1 — The Improver").
> - Describe what in the client's responses points toward this alternative.
> - Be explicit that this is a secondary hypothesis worth exploring in session, not a competing conclusion.
> - Use cautious language.
>
> Example when secondary is present: "Some elements of your responses — particularly your emphasis on 'doing things the right way' and your description of an inner critic that 'never really rests' — also point toward Type 1, The Improver. This doesn't contradict the Nine hypothesis, but it's worth holding as a possibility. In session with Cai, you might explore whether the drive for things to be correct feels primary for you, or whether it's more in service of keeping the peace. This is a useful question, not a contradiction."

### General paragraph-breaking guidance (applies to all four AI-generated client-facing fields)

For any of the AI-generated client-facing fields — `client_narrative`, `core_motivation_evidence`, `instinct_personal_overlay`, `secondary_type_narrative` — if the content naturally runs longer than 4-5 sentences, break it into multiple paragraphs separated by a blank line (`\n\n`). The template will render each segment as a separate `<p>` tag.

Err on the side of more paragraph breaks rather than fewer. Client-facing reading benefits from visual breathing room — long unbroken paragraphs are harder to read on screen, especially for content as dense as type observations. Break at natural beats: after setting up the evidence, after a shift from what's observed to what it suggests, or after any sentence that could stand on its own as a mini-conclusion.

This guidance applies even when a field's primary instruction says "one paragraph." If the paragraph runs long, breaking it is preferred.

## Change 3 — Update the JSON output schema

### Location

Part 4, the JSON schema block.

### Current schema (relevant section)

```
"client_narrative": <string — 3-4 sentences, warm, uses client's words, holds result lightly>,
"coach_note": <string — 2-3 sentences, direct, Enneagram-literate, cites evidence, names one probe>
```

### Proposed schema (relevant section)

```
"client_narrative": <string — 3-4 sentences, warm, opens with specific client detail (not the "based on your responses" framing, which lives in the static report block), uses client's words, holds result lightly>,
"core_motivation_evidence": <string | null — 3-5 sentences pointing to specific evidence in the client's responses that supports the confirmed type's core motivation. null if Stage 4 outcome is AMBIGUOUS or REDIRECT>,
"instinct_personal_overlay": <string | null — 2-4 sentences describing how the client's dominant instinct appears to show up for them specifically. null if Stage 4 outcome is AMBIGUOUS or REDIRECT>,
"secondary_type_narrative": <string | null — 3-5 sentences describing the secondary type candidate. null if holistic_analysis.alternative_type_signal is null>,
"coach_note": <string — 2-3 sentences, direct, Enneagram-literate, cites evidence, names one probe>
```

### Rationale

Each new field is nullable so the schema gracefully handles AMBIGUOUS/REDIRECT outcomes and clients without a meaningful secondary type. The downstream app should check for null before rendering the corresponding section.

## Change 6 — No architectural terms in client-facing AI-generated content

Add the following rule to ALL Task 3 sub-instructions (client_narrative, core_motivation_evidence, instinct_personal_overlay, secondary_type_narrative):

> **Never reference internal assessment architecture in client-facing copy.** This includes: Stage numbers (Stage 1, Stage 2, etc.), framework axis names (Hornevian, Harmonic, Object Relations, Withdrawn, Positive Outlook, etc.), scoring terms, or any language that assumes the client knows how the assessment works. Clients are assumed to be new to the Enneagram. If evidence from a particular answer pattern is relevant, describe what the client said or chose — not which stage or framework axis it came from.

**Example — incorrect:**
> "Stage 2 also showed a Withdrawn social stance and a Positive conflict response, both of which align with the Nine's motivation."

**Example — correct:**
> "Your tendency to step back rather than push forward, and to find the silver lining when conflict arises, aligns closely with the Nine's motivation to preserve harmony."

## Change 7 — Updated "What to Explore" section: new title, new rubric, new Engine instructions

### Title change
"What to Explore With Cai" → "What to Explore With Your Enneagram Coach or Practitioner"

### Intro line change
> "These questions are designed to help you get the most out of your work with a coach or practitioner. Take a moment to sit with each one before your session."

### New rubric for the three exploration questions

The Engine must generate exactly three questions following this rubric. Replace the current Task 5 (or wherever "What to Explore" questions are generated) with the following:

**Question 1 — Core motivation curiosity**
Open with a brief plain-English restatement of the confirmed type's core motivation (one sentence, no jargon). Then invite the client to locate where they feel this most in their life right now, and ask what they would most want to get clearer on with their coach. The question should feel like an invitation, not an interview question.

**Question 2 — Patterns in context**
Ask the client to think of a specific challenge or opportunity they're facing right now — at work or at home. Invite them to notice how their thinking, feeling, and behavior patterns show up in that situation, and whether those patterns are helping, getting in the way, or both. Keep this grounded and concrete — a real situation, not a hypothetical.

**Question 3 — Strengths and challenges choice**
Provide the client with a comma-separated list of their type's key strengths and challenges (draw from the type library's `strengths` and `challenges` arrays — use plain conversational language, not bullet-point labels). Then invite them to choose one — a strength they'd like to bring more of, or a challenge they'd like to work on — and reflect on why that one stands out. The client chooses; the Engine does not choose for them.

### Secondary Type language
Wherever the Engine generates the `secondary_type_narrative` field, replace any instance of "In session with Cai" or similar with "When meeting with an Enneagram coach or practitioner."
